    References: Exponents and Powers

    1. Exponentiation - Wikipedia - Comprehensive article covering integer, rational, and negative exponents along with the identities that make up the laws of exponents, providing the formal mathematical foundation for this chapter.

    2. Nth root - Wikipedia - Detailed explanation of square roots, cube roots, and general nth roots, including their relationship to rational exponents and principal roots, directly supporting the chapter's radicals section.

    3. Scientific notation - Wikipedia - Explains the standard form for writing very large or very small numbers as a coefficient times a power of ten, matching the chapter's scientific notation content and conversion rules.

    4. Big Ideas Math: Algebra 1 (Ron Larson and Laurie Boswell, Big Ideas Learning) - Widely used Algebra 1 textbook with dedicated chapters on exponents, rational exponents, radicals, and polynomial terminology, closely aligned with this chapter's scope and sequence.

    5. Glencoe Algebra 1 (Carter, Cuevas, Day, Malloy, et al., McGraw-Hill Education) - Standard high school Algebra 1 text featuring worked examples on the laws of exponents, negative and zero exponents, scientific notation, and simplifying radical expressions.

    6. Exponents - Math Is Fun - Beginner-friendly explanation of what exponents mean, including how negative exponents represent reciprocals and why any nonzero number to the zero power equals one, reinforcing the chapter's foundational exponent rules.

    7. Laws of Exponents - GeeksforGeeks - Clear walkthrough of the product, quotient, power, zero-exponent, and negative-exponent rules with worked examples, directly matching the chapter's Laws of Exponents section.

    8. Scientific Notation - Math Is Fun - Step-by-step guide to converting numbers to and from scientific notation, with real-world examples of very large and very small quantities, supporting the chapter's scientific notation lessons.

    9. Radicals - Purplemath - Explains radical notation, the index and radicand, principal square and cube roots, and the technique of factoring out perfect powers to simplify radicals, aligning with the chapter's roots and radicals content.

    10. Polynomials - Math Is Fun - Introduces polynomial terminology including monomials, binomials, and trinomials with clear examples, supporting the chapter's introduction to polynomial classification by number of terms.
